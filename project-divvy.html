01 · Business challenge

How can casual riders become the next generation of annual members?

Divvy wants to understand how casual riders differ from annual members and identify opportunities to increase membership adoption.

By analyzing ride frequency, duration, bike type, and days of activity, this project surfaces practical ways to improve conversion, retention, and recurring revenue.

03 · Insight one

Members drive the majority of trips

Annual members generated 320,152 Q1 rides, representing 76.65% of total ride volume.

Conversion potential

Casual riders accounted for 97,504 rides—a smaller share, but a valuable audience for targeted membership messaging.

The opportunity is not simply more trips; it is reaching engaged casual riders with a membership proposition that matches their use of the service.

04 · Insight two

Casual riders take longer trips

Average casual rides lasted 17.4 minutes versus 10.5 minutes for annual members.

Marketing implication

Casual riders spend 66% more time per ride on average, indicating a stronger recreational, sightseeing, or leisure use case.

Membership messaging can emphasize value, flexibility, and unlimited access for longer rides.

05 · Insight three

Casual riders favor e-bikes

Bike type preference differs by membership type, creating a clear opportunity for tailored marketing.

Rider preference

54% of casual rides used electric bikes, while members slightly favored classic bikes.

E-bike availability and reduced unlock costs are benefits that can make annual membership more compelling for casual riders.

06 · Insight four

Casual riders peak on Fridays and Saturdays

End-of-week activity makes Fridays and Saturdays natural moments for targeted membership campaigns.

Campaign timing

Casual riders reached their highest activity on Saturday (19,776 rides), closely followed by Friday (19,484 rides).

Weekend offers and recreational-riding campaigns should appear when casual riders are most active.
